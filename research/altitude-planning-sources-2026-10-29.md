# Banff elevation and journey planning — October 29, 2026

## Scope and repair

Replaces the existing `/altitude-guide/` within its established identity. Owned files are that page, `src/data/altitudePlanning.ts`, `src/components/AltitudePlanningInteractions.astro`, `scripts/check-altitude-planning.mjs` and this record. No shared hotel/trail/lake data, affiliate identifiers, registry, workflow, tools directory, account or production setting changed by this author.

The previous page assigned Low/Moderate/Very Low medical risk from three home-elevation buckets and promised readiness for any trail or alpine passes after a fixed number of days. It also confused Lake Louise community with its lakeshore, mixed Calgary and Denver into the same numerical band, supplied an unsupported 1,800 m cutoff, fixed fluid/medication/recovery instructions and a symptom classification that understated coordination problems. Its FAQ schema lacked matching visible FAQs. The unsupported risk model, home-city fields, prescribed schedule, medication/dosing copy and unidentified hero photograph are removed.

The replacement makes five concrete journey comparisons from nine explicitly named publisher points. It shows endpoint subtraction only, with a prominent explanation that this is not hiking ascent, distance, travel time, effort, a medical score or route permission. It asks about an overnight **plan**, not a person's medical circumstances or an assumed sleeping elevation. There are no free-text health fields, symptoms input, eligibility result or medication instructions.

## Health and emergency primary sources

All URLs read October 29, 2026. General health information is identified by publisher; this is not a clinician-authored personalized assessment.

- [CDC Yellow Book, 2026 edition: High-Altitude Travel and Altitude Illness](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html), published April 23, 2025. Used for the general relevance of sleeping elevation, the stated 2,450 m-and-sometimes-lower context for unacclimatized sleepers, the difference between a high day visit and a lower overnight, and the lack of a simple predictive screen or fitness protection. No numerical risk table, individualized risk group, medication, dose or automatic acclimatization schedule is reproduced.
- [MyHealth Alberta: Altitude Sickness](https://myhealth.alberta.ca/HEALTH/Pages/conditions.aspx?hwid=ug3357&lang=en-ca), adapted/reviewed by Alberta Health Services. Its displayed adaptation date is retained as `12/10/2025`, without interpreting the ambiguous numeric date format. Used for possible symptoms, overlap with other illness, stopping further ascent, deterioration/descent and not leaving a severely ill person alone. No drug recommendations or promised time to recovery copied.
- [NHS: Altitude sickness](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/altitude-sickness/). Used for gradual travel, avoiding dehydration/alcohol during higher travel, and urgent signs including confusion, coordination difficulty, resting breathlessness and difficulty waking. Its UK emergency routes are not transplanted to Canada. General symptom information does not diagnose a visitor.
- [Alberta Health Services: Health Link 811](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/findhealth/service.aspx?Id=1001957). Health advice/care navigation is clearly separate from emergency rescue. The page does not promise that a phone call confirms travel readiness or that every virtual service is available to every visitor.
- [Parks Canada: Mountain search and rescue](https://www.parks.canada.ca/pn-np/mtn/securiteenmontagne-mountainsafety/programme-program/res-sar). Canadian emergency handoff: 911, identify the Mountain National Park and exact location, ask for the Park Rescue Team; `403-762-4506` is identified in its satellite-phone context. Cell/device limitations and uncertain rescue access remain explicit. Also cross-checked [Banff contact page](https://www.parcs.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/info/contact), which gives 911 for emergencies and 403-762-1470 for non-emergencies. The non-emergency number is not promoted as a medical emergency line.

The page provides a short serious-illness response and professional advice route; it does not reproduce a self-diagnostic symptom severity calculator. Descent is framed with emergency assistance/instructions, not an instruction to send a confused person alone down technical terrain.
